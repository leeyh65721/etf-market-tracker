# World Bank October 2026 Commodity Markets Outlook — new official supply and inflation forecasts

**Independent research check:** 2026-10-29 14:35 KST (05:35 UTC). **Official release:** 2026-10-28 (World Bank press release). Public macro-market research only; this is not a personal portfolio record, trading instruction, or a claim of continuously monitored quotes.

## Newly confirmed primary-source data
- The World Bank's October 28 Commodity Markets Outlook forecasts **overall commodity prices +23% in calendar 2026**, followed by **-7% in 2027**.
- **Energy prices +32% in 2026**; annual average Brent **$91/bbl in 2026**, versus **$69 in 2025**, with a baseline **$79 in 2027**. These are annual averages/forecasts, **not spot or futures quotes**.
- **Global oil supply -5.2% in 2026** (largest fall in more than four decades except 2020); **oil consumption -2.5%**. Supply is forecast below consumption, with inventory drawdowns and greater vulnerability to incremental outages.
- The report assumes no major further conflict escalation. In a combined adverse scenario, **2027 annual-average Brent $105/bbl** and food commodity prices 12% above the baseline; these are **scenario projections, not observed prices**.
- Food commodities forecast +10% in 2026 and +7% in 2027, a cumulative +18% (not the arithmetic sum).
- Independent Reuters coverage dated 2026-10-28 corroborates the key forecast magnitudes.

## What differs from earlier recorded market events
Prior events and daily notes discussed high oil prices, regional attacks, central-bank inflation risks and specific corporate results. This World Bank publication is **new quantitative official global outlook evidence** about projected annual commodity inflation and the scale of oil supply contraction. It is **not** a newly confirmed physical attack, a sudden real-time supply outage, or a newly observed oil-price spike. No claim that these projections alone changed market prices or overturned a prior ETF allocation decision.

## Transmission, contrary evidence, and conditions
- **Potential transmission:** persistent oil/gas and food input costs -> inflation persistence -> reduced room for policy easing / higher real discount rates -> valuation pressure on long-duration technology and other equities; energy-intensive consumer companies face cost and demand pressure; credit quality can weaken if costs persist.
- **Counterevidence:** 2027 baseline commodity prices forecast to fall 7%; diplomatic talks could reduce geopolitical premia; supply recovery or lower demand can temper inflation. On 2026-10-29 at 03:51 UTC Reuters reported Brent futures $114.10 and WTI futures $101.45, both down over 1% that session, not a fresh price surge.
- **Structural vs temporary:** forecast points to prolonged supply vulnerability but does not establish imminent credit or funding-market stress. Reassess on confirmed shipping/production outages, inflation surprises, sharp sustained yield/credit-spread repricing, or official forecast revisions; ease concern if verified inventories, shipments, and prices recover.
- **Urgency:** material background for the next scheduled portfolio review, **not** independently sufficient for emergency reallocation, immediate ETF purchases/sales, or a push alert. No execution of trades.
