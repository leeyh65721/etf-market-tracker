# Tokyo Electron FY2027 guidance: semiconductor equipment demand counter-evidence (2026-10-30 KST)

- **Event:** Tokyo Electron (8035) announced FY2027 H1 results and first full-year guidance after the Tokyo market close on **2026-10-30 15:30 JST/KST**. Independently covered by Reuters on 2026-10-30 08:15 UTC (17:15 KST).
- **Verification:** Official Tokyo Electron FY2027 Q2 consolidated results PDF (2026-10-30), company IR page, and Reuters 2026-10-30. Checked around 19:40 KST. This is a company release and forward guidance, not a verified post-announcement US market response.
- **H1 realized results (April–September 2026):** net sales **¥1,667.455bn (+41.3% YoY)**; operating income **¥506.776bn (+67.2% YoY)**; operating cash flow **+¥469.934bn** (prior-year H1 about +¥250.849bn). H1 sales exceeded the previous company forecast by 2.9% and operating income by 10.6%.
- **FY2027 ending March 2027 guidance (not realized):** sales **¥3,700bn (+51.4% YoY)**; operating income **¥1,169bn (+87.1% YoY)**; net income attributable to owners **¥865bn (+50.6% YoY)**. Management attributes the outlook to customers' semiconductor capex associated with AI server expansion. This is an initial full-year forecast after previously withholding one, **not** an 87% upward revision versus a previous FY2027 estimate.
- **Difference from existing analysis:** 2026-10-30 daily analysis and the 2026-10-30 05:32 Amazon/Intel event highlighted hyperscaler free-cash-flow/ROI risks and Intel margin guidance. Tokyo Electron's actual H1 sales, profit and cash-flow growth plus its new forecast offer **concrete supplier-side counter-evidence** against an indiscriminate collapse in semiconductor-equipment demand. Previous 10/28 ASM, SK Hynix, and other supplier reports also showed resilience; avoid presenting generic 'AI demand is strong' as new evidence.
- **Transmission:** Continued customer semiconductor equipment spending may support the global equipment supply chain and potentially semiconductor sentiment, including US semiconductor ETFs. It does **not** prove AI hyperscaler investments will generate adequate returns, nor prove the order book and cash flow of every US-listed SOXX constituent. Rising oil/natural gas, inflation and bond yields could still pressure long-duration tech valuations; risk-off moves can coexist with robust supplier operating results.
- **Counterarguments/limitations:** Company-specific product mix and backlog may not generalize; guidance can be revised; customer investment and ultimate monetization are different questions. Tokyo Electron is not treated as a direct SOXX holding. US 2026-10-30 regular-session price discovery has not occurred as of the check. The latest available FRED ICE US high-yield OAS **3.14%** and investment-grade OAS **0.86%** are both **2026-10-28**, so a contemporaneous credit-market response cannot be established. Price feeds are delayed and the check is not continuous real-time surveillance.
- **Urgency / action:** **No emergency ETF allocation change or trade recommendation.** Positive company fundamentals do not negate the existing AI capex ROI thesis or justify chasing a premarket move. Reassess if multiple independent equipment suppliers report materially weaker *realized* orders, shipments, margins or cash flow, or if US semiconductor market breadth and credit conditions decisively change. Ordinary portfolio rebalancing belongs to the weekly review.
