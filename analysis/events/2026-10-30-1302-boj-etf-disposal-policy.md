# BOJ unexpectedly accelerates ETF disposal; policy rate held — structural Japan liquidity watch

**Checked:** 2026-10-30 13:02 KST (04:02 UTC). **Decision:** Bank of Japan 2026-10-30 monetary policy meeting; Reuters ETF-specific report timestamp 2026-10-30 03:34 UTC (12:34 KST). Public market research only. No personal portfolio or order data.

## New evidence, separate from earlier AI/semiconductor and copper events
- The BOJ **held its short-term policy rate at 1.25%** (8-1 decision), broadly expected. Board member Hajime Takata proposed 1.50%, rejected. BOJ said underlying inflation is broadly consistent with its 2% target and revised its forecast for core inflation in the fiscal year ending March 2028 from **2.4% to 2.6%**.
- **New and unexpected policy:** BOJ will accelerate sales of its *Japanese equity ETF holdings* from about **JPY 330 billion to JPY 860 billion per year at book value** (~2.61x), **effective January 2027**, at a scale of about **0.1% of Japanese equity market trading value**. Estimated completion period falls from **112 years to 43 years**. This is not a sale of US-listed SOXX or a 2026 immediate liquidation.
- The hold and potential December rate hike had already been widely expected; **the change in ETF disposal pace is the newly confirmed policy action**. It does not independently explain the prior 2026-10-29 US tech selloff or the 2026-10-30 Asia AI-stock selloff.

## Cross-market pathways, counterevidence and timing
- Japanese equity supply/market liquidity: the central bank will be a larger, gradual net seller from 2027, creating a structural (not sudden) Japanese equity supply headwind. Stronger inflation normalization could also lift JGB yields and the yen, potentially affecting cross-border funding/carry trades, global growth valuations and US ETF risk appetite **if** accompanied by abrupt yen appreciation and credit stress. No such chain is established solely by today's announcement.
- Reuters 2026-10-30 Asian-market observation: USD/JPY briefly reached **157.115**, dollar index **101.77**, US 10-year Treasury yield **4.8958%**; prices are not simultaneous or necessarily a causal reaction to the BOJ ETF sales. Reuters and analysts describe the sale pace as gradual and likely limited in market impact.
- Countercase: BOJ estimates ETF sales at only ~0.1% of Japan's market turnover, implementation starts January 2027, rate hold was expected, and a rapid forced deleveraging or funding freeze is unconfirmed. Japan's tech-stock losses largely followed already documented US AI capex concerns.
- Official delayed US credit/volatility observations **as of 2026-10-28 close**: ICE BofA HY OAS **3.14%**, IG OAS **0.86%**, VIX **16.91**; cannot establish post-decision credit stability. US 2026-10-29 credit closes not yet available in FRED as of check. Same-time USD/KRW, 2-year/real yields, copper, gold, gas, short-term funding, market breadth and ETF spreads not fully validated.
- Existing 2026-10-30 daily analysis had a data-quality ERROR and existing 2026-10-30 Amazon/Intel and Oyu Tolgoi events are distinct. Do not repeat them as new BOJ evidence.

## Urgency, ETF relevance and withdrawal
- **Classification:** meaningful new central-bank balance-sheet policy, **not** verified urgent US-listed ETF portfolio reallocation or systemic liquidity shock. No new ETF recommendation; no order.
- **Escalate if:** BOJ unexpectedly front-loads sales before 2027, raises rates earlier/more than anticipated, USD/JPY or JGB yields move abruptly with confirmed cross-asset deleveraging, or HY/IG spreads and funding rates show correlated stress.
- **De-escalate if:** sales follow the announced 2027 gradual schedule, Japan equity liquidity absorbs supply, yen/JGB volatility subsides, and no US credit/funding spillover is observed.
- **Next:** Governor Ueda's briefing at **2026-10-30 06:30 UTC (15:30 KST)**, Japan market closing data, US Oct 29 credit/volatility release and Oct 30 US session. No continuous real-time quote monitoring; prices may be delayed.
